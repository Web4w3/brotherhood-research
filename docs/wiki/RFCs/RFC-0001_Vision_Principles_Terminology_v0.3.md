# RFC-0001: Vision, Principles and Terminology

**Status:** Draft

**Version:** 0.3

---

# Abstract

The Open Federation of Minds Model (OFMM) defines a framework for collaborative deliberation between Humans, Artificial Intelligences, and Hybrid Human-AI participants.

Unlike traditional AI systems focused on automation, orchestration, or tool execution, OFMM focuses on collective reasoning and transparent decision support.

OFMM enables different forms of intelligence to participate as equal contributors within a shared deliberation process.

---

# 1. Motivation

Modern enterprise decisions rarely involve a single person or a single AI system.

A typical architectural, security, legal, financial, or strategic decision requires contributions from multiple stakeholders.

Examples include:

- Executives
- Enterprise Architects
- Developers
- Security Specialists
- Legal Advisors
- Compliance Officers
- Artificial Intelligence Systems

Current collaboration is fragmented across:

- meetings;
- emails;
- chat systems;
- ticketing systems;
- isolated AI conversations.

As a result:

- context is lost;
- decisions become difficult to trace;
- assumptions remain hidden;
- experts are involved too late;
- bottlenecks are hard to identify.

OFMM addresses these limitations by introducing a shared deliberation model.

---

# 2. Vision

OFMM assumes that future decision making will involve multiple forms of intelligence working together.

These intelligences may be:

- Human;
- Artificial;
- Hybrid.

The goal of OFMM is not to replace humans.

The goal of OFMM is to improve collective intelligence.

---

# 3. Problem Statement

Most AI systems assume the following interaction model:

```text
Question
   ↓
AI
   ↓
Answer
```

This model works well for individual tasks.

It does not adequately support:

- enterprise decision making;
- expert collaboration;
- governance;
- risk management;
- accountability.

Real-world decisions require deliberation rather than answer generation.

---

# 4. Core Principles

## Principle 1: Deliberation First

The primary object of OFMM is a deliberation.

OFMM standardizes discussion and reasoning rather than task execution.

## Principle 2: Mind Members

Every participant in OFMM is represented as a Mind Member.

A Mind Member may be:

- Human
- Artificial
- Hybrid

## Principle 3: Human Participation

Humans are participants of the reasoning process rather than observers of AI outputs.

## Principle 4: Human-in-the-Loop

OFMM assumes that complex and consequential decisions benefit from continuous human involvement.

## Principle 5: Universal Challengeability

No participant is considered infallible.

Every proposal may be challenged.

## Principle 6: Transparency

The state of a deliberation must be visible to authorized participants.

## Principle 7: Dynamic Membership

Participants may join an active deliberation when additional expertise is required.

## Principle 8: Asynchronous Collaboration

OFMM does not assume real-time participation.

---

# 5. Terminology

## Mind Member

An entity capable of contributing reasoning, knowledge, expertise, proposals, analysis, criticism, or decisions.

## Human Mind Member

A participant whose reasoning is performed by one or more humans.

## Artificial Mind Member

A participant whose reasoning is performed entirely by AI systems.

## Hybrid Mind Member

A participant whose reasoning emerges from collaboration between humans and AI.

## Deliberation

A structured discussion among Mind Members.

## Proposal

A suggested interpretation, recommendation, solution, or course of action.

## Challenge

A constructive disagreement intended to improve reasoning quality.

## Consensus

A sufficient level of agreement allowing progress toward a decision.

## Decision

An accepted outcome of a deliberation.

## Bottleneck

A participant, dependency, or process preventing further progress.

# Mission Statement

> OFMM exists to improve collective intelligence by enabling Human, Artificial, and Hybrid Mind Members to reason together within a transparent deliberation process.
